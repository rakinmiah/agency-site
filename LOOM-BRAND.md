## The lineage (why this name has unusual depth)
- **Loom = "the tool."** Old English *gelōma*: simply the essential implement. Loomwork ≈ the
  work done with the essential tool.
- **Oldest machine, oldest guild.** Warp-weighted looms ~7,000 years old; loom weights in nearly
  every Iron Age/Saxon British household. The Weavers' Company (1155) is England's oldest
  recorded craft guild. Medieval England's wealth WAS the wool trade (the Woolsack).
- **The loomworkers were the clients, eight generations early.** Handloom weaving = the
  archetypal cottage industry: the loomshop upstairs, weavers' windows, a named family trading
  on reputation in its town. Today's trades are the last cottage industries. (Shadow version:
  handloom weavers vs the power looms — us on the side of the makers against template factories.)
- **The loom invented the computer.** Jacquard's punch-card loom (1804) → Babbage → Lovelace:
  "the Analytical Engine weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard-loom weaves flowers
  and leaves." Punch cards ran computing into the 1970s. Software descends from the loom.
- **"Web" was weaving's word first.** OE *webb* = woven cloth; a weaver was a *webster*. The
  WWW borrowed the loom's vocabulary. A web studio named after the loom is the word coming home.
- **Wyrd inheritance.** The owner's original name instinct (wyrd = fate you weave) survives
  here with a radio-safe spelling: outcomes woven, not wished for.

## The sales metaphor (use this in pitches regardless of final name)
**Warp and weft.** Warp = the strong threads fixed first = THE SITE (built once, structural).
Weft = the thread run through continuously = SEARCH/ADS/CONTENT (ongoing). Separately: string.
Woven: cloth. One image sells the build + retainer model to any tradesperson.

## House vs Work
-house = the PLACE (institution, warm, Bakehouse family, "a studio you could visit").
-work  = the ACT/OUTPUT (graft register; the page's own language — nav "Work",
         "Built the way we build yours").
