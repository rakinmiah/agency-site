# LOOMWORK — the meaning, and the brief for the final mark

## The one-line meaning
**Loomwork is the work done on the loom — and the loom is the oldest machine we have for
turning loose threads into something that holds together.** That is the business: taking a
local trade's scattered presence — a phone number, some reviews, a half-dead website, word
of mouth — and weaving it into one thing that carries weight and brings work in.

## The story, in the order it should be told

**1. The word means "the tool."**
Old English *gelōma* — an implement, the essential piece of kit in a working household.
Before *loom* meant a weaving frame it just meant **the thing you work with**. So Loomwork,
at its root, is *work done with the essential tool*. Not decoration. Equipment.

**2. It is the oldest machine, and the oldest trade.**
Warp-weighted looms go back ~7,000 years; loom weights are among the most common objects
dug out of British soil. Weaving predates writing. The Weavers' Company, chartered 1155, is
the oldest recorded craft guild in England. Medieval England's wealth *was* wool — the
Lord Speaker still sits on a Woolsack. When you say "loomwork" in Britain you are invoking
the trade that paid for the country.

**3. The loomworkers were our clients, eight generations early.**
Before factories, weaving was the archetypal cottage industry: the loomshop upstairs in the
weaver's own house, the long "weavers' windows" still visible in Lancashire, Yorkshire and
Spitalfields. A named family, working from home, living on its reputation in one town.
That is exactly the plumber, the taxi firm, the gas engineer — the last cottage industries.
(The shadow: handloom weavers were undercut and destroyed by the power looms of the
Industrial Revolution. We are on the side of the handloom weavers, against the template
factories and the AI slop.)

**4. The loom invented the computer.**
Jacquard's punch-card loom (1804) encoded its pattern in holes. Babbage lifted the idea for
the Analytical Engine, and Ada Lovelace wrote the founding sentence of the industry:
*"the Analytical Engine weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard-loom weaves flowers
and leaves."* Punch cards ran computing into the 1970s. Software descends from the loom —
loomwork → card-work → code-work → this.

**5. "Web" was weaving's word first.**
Old English *webb* meant woven cloth. A weaver was a *webster* — every Webster alive is
descended from the trade. The World Wide Web borrowed the loom's whole vocabulary: web,
threads, spinning. **A web studio named after the loom is the word coming home.**

**6. Warp and weft is the business model.**
Cloth needs two threads. The **warp** is fixed to the frame first — strong, structural, done
once: *that is the website*. The **weft** runs through it continuously, and it is the weft
that actually makes cloth: *that is search, ads, and the ongoing care*. Separately they are
string. Woven, they are cloth. This is how the build + retainer is explained to a
tradesperson in one image they will not forget.

## Voice that falls out of it
Plain, made, counted. "Built" not "crafted". "Work" not "solutions". Never mystical about
the weaving — it is a tool metaphor, not a spiritual one. British, direct, unshowy.

## What is already decided (the mark must live with these)
- **Wordmark:** LOOMWORK, Anton, tight tracking, set edge-to-edge at hero scale.
- **Incumbent mark:** CONTINUOUS — the two O's replaced by one continuous rounded loop
  (one unbroken thread through the name). Verified legible to 14px.
- **Colour:** ink `#0B0B0D` / cream `#F1ECE2` / studio amber `#D99A4E`
  (light-ground sibling `#8B6332`). The loop currently carries the amber.
- **Register:** premium, restrained, editorial. No mysticism, no cottage-craft whimsy,
  no gradients, no mascots.
